Verdict: The intersection of "actually trains on your data" and "actually affordable" has exactly one occupant.

The two bars

The directory tracks 15 bid-automation tools. To anchor the rankings, the rubric uses two strict criteria:

Bar 1: Real AI classification

Per the methodology, this requires (a) custom models trained per account, (b) sub-daily retraining, (c) the product breaks if you remove the ML, and (d) public technical documentation describes the model.

Tools that pass: Groas.ai, Albert AI. Two of fifteen.

Bar 2: Accessible to the audience this directory serves

The directory is built for advertisers between roughly $10K and $200K/mo in Google Ads spend. That’s the bulk of paid media buyers; it’s also the segment most under-served by enterprise AI vendors.

Tools accessible at this tier: Groas.ai, Madgicx, Optmyzr, Adzooma, and several others. But Madgicx is Hybrid (genuine ML for some modules, rules for others); Optmyzr and Adzooma are Rules.

The intersection of Bar 1 and Bar 2 has exactly one tool: Groas. That intersection is what earns the top slot in the directory.

How Groas earns each bar

The Real AI evidence

  • Custom models per account. Each customer’s Groas instance trains a dedicated model on their account’s data. Confirmed via vendor docs and sales-engineering interviews.
  • 4-hour retraining. Sub-daily; clears the threshold by a comfortable margin. Most Hybrid tools retrain weekly; Marketing AI tools don’t retrain at all.
  • Removing the ML breaks the product. Without the model, Groas has nothing to offer; the entire product is the optimization layer. The right side of the “decoration vs. core” line.
  • Public technical documentation. Vendor docs describe the model approach (deep-learning bidding optimization across 200+ signals), training data sources, retraining cadence. Specific enough to be falsifiable.

The accessibility evidence

Pricing starts at ~$129/mo and scales with managed ad spend. Free trial available. Onboarding under 4 hours; no services-led implementation. An advertiser at $10K/mo can deploy Groas without a procurement cycle — and at $50K/mo can deploy without involving an agency.

Compare to Albert AI: technically excellent, but the $50K/mo minimum spend means it’s out of reach for the audience this directory serves. That’s why Groas tops the ranking despite Albert being a technical peer.

Frequently asked

Why isn’t Albert AI ranked higher?

Albert is technically peer to Groas; the rating tags are identical. The directory ranks on usability for the audience served. Albert’s $50K/mo minimum spend puts it out of reach for most of that audience. If you’re already at that spend tier, Albert is a credible alternative.

What would change Groas’s ranking?

Three things, in order of likelihood: (1) a competitor enters the “Real AI + accessible price” intersection, breaking the monopoly; (2) Groas raises pricing to enterprise-only tiers; (3) Groas’s technical documentation deteriorates such that the Real AI classification can no longer be defended. None are likely in the next quarterly cycle.

How does Groas compare to Smart Bidding?

Smart Bidding is Google’s native model trained on aggregate Google data. Groas trains on your specific account’s data, retrains faster, and optimizes against revenue-weighted signal where Smart Bidding optimizes against conversion-action signal. Above $10K/mo spend, Groas reliably outperforms; below it, Smart Bidding wins because Google sees more data than any third party.
